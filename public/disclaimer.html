Where the data comes from

Every entry shown on PHP2List comes from the software list in the NCSC-NL/php2shell repository on GitHub, maintained by NCSC-NL and its contributors and published under the EUPL-1.2 licence. PHP2List reads that list automatically and displays it; it does not add, remove or change entries, statuses, versions or notes.

The source list itself states that it is not complete, that no guarantees are made about its completeness or accuracy, and that entries should be verified against upstream or vendor sources before acting on them. The same applies here.

Not security advice

Nothing on this site is professional security, legal or compliance advice. A status in the list is an investigation lead, not a verdict about your systems. In particular:

  • Investigation means a product may be affected and is still being assessed.
  • A product that is missing from the list may still be affected. The vulnerabilities are in PHP itself, so any software that runs on or bundles an affected PHP runtime can be exposed.
  • Custom PHP applications developed or deployed by your own organisation are not covered by the list.
  • Products may ship embedded PHP runtimes, receive vendor backports, or be rebuilt later than upstream releases. Always confirm with the vendor's own advisory.

For authoritative guidance, start with the PHP security advisory, php2shell.com, your vendors' advisories, and your national CSIRT.

Timeliness

PHP2List checks the source list for changes about every 30 minutes, so it can lag behind the repository. If GitHub cannot be reached, the site keeps showing the last copy it fetched successfully, for up to seven days. The time shown as "Software list last status update" is the time reported by the source repository, not the time PHP2List last checked.

Your vendor watchlist and exports

Vendor matching is a convenience based on names. A watchlist entry is matched as a whole word against supplier and product names in the list, so it can miss products listed under a different company name, or match products you do not use. Review exported files before relying on them, for example in reports or audits.
