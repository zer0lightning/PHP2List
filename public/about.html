About PHP2List

Why this site exists, what it's useful for, and what it isn't.

PHP2List, created by zer0lightning, is an independent, unofficial website that makes the public NCSC-NL php2shell software list easier to search, filter, share and export during the response to the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities.

Why it exists

When PHP2Shell was disclosed, NCSC-NL and its contributors began publishing a valuable inventory of software that depends on PHP, with a status for each product. It's published as 27 alphabetical Markdown pages on GitHub, which works well for maintaining it, but is hard to use when you need a quick answer:

  • You can't search across all the pages at once, or filter by status.
  • There's no easy way to keep an eye on just the vendors and products your organisation actually uses.
  • Getting the data into a spreadsheet or ticket for your team takes manual work.
  • It's hard to see the bigger picture: how many listed products are fixed, and how fast that's changing.

PHP2List adds a fast, searchable view on top of the same data, without changing it, so defenders can spend less time looking things up and more time patching.

What you can use it for

  • Check your software: search by supplier, product, version or note text, and filter by status for either CVE.
  • Track what you run: build a watchlist of exact suppliers, exact products or keywords, and see only those.
  • Share with your team: send a link that opens the same watchlist and filters, or bookmark it to come back to.
  • Feed your process: export to CSV or JSON for asset inventories, tickets or reports, including a per-vendor summary.
  • Follow progress: the patch progress page shows how many listed products are fixed or still open since disclosure.
  • Brief others: the status overview and charts give a quick picture for colleagues or management.

What it is not

  • Not an official source. PHP2List is not NCSC-NL or php2shell.com and is not affiliated with or endorsed by them, or by the PHP project. It only displays NCSC-NL's public list.
  • Not a vulnerability scanner. It doesn't check your servers. To know whether you're exposed, check the PHP version actually running on each system and your vendors' advisories.
  • Not a complete inventory. The list covers products someone has added to it. A product that isn't listed may still be affected, and your own custom PHP applications aren't covered at all.
  • Not a verdict. Statuses come from the source list and are investigation leads. PHP2List doesn't verify vendor claims.
  • Not security advice, and not a support service. See the disclaimer. Corrections to the list belong with NCSC-NL.
  • Not collecting your data. Your watchlist stays in your browser; see the privacy notice.

How it works

  • The site reads the list from the NCSC-NL repository on GitHub about every 30 minutes and shows it with the same columns: Supplier, Product, Version, the status for each CVE, Notes and Links.
  • Nothing in the data is added, removed or edited. If GitHub can't be reached, the last copy is shown with a clear warning.
  • The patch progress charts are rebuilt from the list's own changelog.
  • The site loads nothing from other websites and has no analytics or tracking.

Open source

  • PHP2List's code is open source under the MIT licence: PHP2List on GitHub. You're free to use, copy, modify and run your own copy.
  • The software list is not part of PHP2List's code. It's © NCSC-NL and contributors under the EUPL-1.2 licence (TLP:CLEAR), and those terms also apply to the data in exports.
  • If you run your own copy, it can read from a fork or mirror of the list if the original ever becomes unavailable.

About PHP2List

A searchable view of the software inventory published by NCSC-NL and contributors in the php2shell repository, licensed under EUPL-1.2. The list refreshes automatically from GitHub.

The vulnerabilities were found by researchers at Wiz Research, OpenAI and Calif. Read the full story at php2shell.com and see our acknowledgements.

Accuracy is not guaranteed; use at your own risk. No support is offered; list corrections go to NCSC-NL. See the disclaimer.

Status labels

  • Investigation May be affected; being assessed.
  • Vulnerable Confirmed vulnerable in the stated scope.
  • Fix The listed version contains a fix.
  • Workaround Vulnerable; mitigation available.
  • Not vuln Confirmed not vulnerable.

The Version column relates to the status: known vulnerable versions, or the version that introduced the fix.
